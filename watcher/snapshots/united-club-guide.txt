United Club Access: The Fare Bucket That Decides Which Lounge You Get
A Polaris Base ticket opens a United Club but not a United Polaris lounge. Every published route into each, what membership costs and the guest rules.
United lists Base, standard or flexible Polaris fares as eligible for United Club, but only standard and flexible fares for the United Polaris lounge, so a Polaris Base ticket buys the lie-flat seat and the club and stops at the Polaris lounge door.
Individual membership costs $750 or 94,000 miles at every MileagePlus status tier and permits no guests and no Star Alliance lounges; All Access starts at $1,400 and falls to $1,100 for Global Services members.

United runs two different airport lounges, and the thing that decides which one you can enter is not your cabin. It is your fare bucket.
A Polaris ticket in the Base fare gets you into a United Club. It does not get you into a United Polaris lounge. Both facts sit in the same access table on United's own site, one row apart, and the distinction is invisible at the point of booking.
This guide covers the two lounge types, every published route into each, what membership costs, and the guest rules, which are stricter than most people expect. Everything below was read on United's own lounge access page and its membership page on September 4, 2026.
United Club is the standard network. United describes it as "our standard club offering" with "free food, drinks, comfortable seats and lots of outlets," and puts the count at 45-plus locations worldwide.
United Polaris lounges are the premium tier, which United describes as "exclusive lounges created for travelers with standard and flexible fares in United Polaris and premium Star Alliance cabins," offering "shower suites, sit-down meals and private relaxation areas."
Note the wording on the Polaris lounge: standard and flexible fares. That single qualifier is the most consequential detail on the page.
Set the two access tables side by side and the gap appears.
For United Club access, United lists "Base, standard or flexible fare in United Polaris" as eligible, with a same-day boarding pass for a US transcontinental, long-haul international or Hawaii flight.
For United Polaris lounge access, United lists "Standard or flexible fare in United Polaris." Base is absent.
So a Polaris Base ticket buys the lie-flat seat and the United Club, and stops at the Polaris lounge door. The cabin is identical. The fare bucket is not.
This is the same structural move Delta made with Basic Business, and it is worth recognizing as a pattern rather than a quirk: airlines are unbundling lounge access from premium cabins by fare code, where a booking screen will not show it to you. If the lounge is part of why you are paying for the cabin, the fare bucket is now a thing to confirm.
United's table lists these, and the guest column is where the surprises are.
United Polaris, any fare including Base. Same-day boarding pass for a US transcontinental, long-haul international or Hawaii flight in Polaris. Access at departure, connecting and arrival airports. No guests allowed.
United Business on international flights. A boarding pass for travel in United Business. United adds that "customers traveling to and from Canada in United First are eligible for United Business access." Departure, connecting and arrival airports. No guests allowed.
First class on a Star Alliance member airline. A boarding pass in first class on a Star Alliance carrier, at the departure airport for that international flight.
A United Club membership. Covered in detail below.
A one-time pass. A valid pass plus a same-day boarding pass on a United, Star Alliance or contracted partner flight. Guests are not allowed, and access is subject to availability.
Two restrictions on one-time passes are easy to miss. United states that "starting May 1, 2025, one-time passholders may use their pass only within three hours of their scheduled flight departure," and that this "doesn't apply if the pass is being used during a connection." Passes issued by MileagePlus credit cards "must be redeemed by the primary cardmember, an authorized user or guests accompanied by either," so a pass cannot simply be handed to somebody else.
United also states a general rule worth quoting: "You can only use your own United Club membership card and same-day boarding passes to access our clubs. We may ask you to show a government-issued photo ID for verification."
United sells two memberships, and they differ on guests rather than on locations.
Individual membership covers "access to 45+ United Club locations worldwide for membership holders." United states plainly that individual members "can't bring guests or visit Star Alliance partner lounges."
All Access membership covers the same 45-plus locations, plus "up to two adult guests or one adult and any dependents under 18," plus Star Alliance partner lounges.

Individual membership costs $750 no matter what your status is. Every row is identical. Status buys a discount on All Access and nothing at all on Individual, which means elite status and the cheaper membership do not interact.
United Club membership comes with eligible MileagePlus credit cards, and the guest rules differ again. Our United Club Card page has the card's terms.
United states that "United Club cardmembers can bring one adult guest and any dependents under 18," which is better than Individual membership and short of All Access. And cardmembers "with Premier Gold status or $50,000 in purchases with their card in a calendar year unlock All Access membership benefits."
That $50,000 threshold is the interesting number, because it is a spending target rather than a flying one. It is the only route to All Access that does not require either buying the more expensive membership or holding Premier Gold.
The United Club Card is the card that carries the membership. Lower down the lineup, United's lounge page notes two United Club one-time passes with the United Explorer Card, and the United Quest Card sits between them. United sets out the membership benefit that comes with these cards on its own United Club and lounges page, and our comparison of lounge-access cards sets them against the issuer networks, which reach far more airports than United's own.
Access is narrower, and the guest rules are inconsistent in an interesting way.
Standard or flexible fare in United Polaris. Same-day boarding pass. Lounges at departure, connecting and arrival airports along the eligible same-day itinerary. No guests.
First class on Lufthansa, Swiss or ANA. Same-day boarding pass, at the departure airport for that international long-haul first class flight. One guest allowed.
Business class on ANA, Air New Zealand or ITA, and Basic or Flex business class on Lufthansa, Swiss, Austrian or Brussels Airlines. Same-day boarding pass, departure airport. No guests.
Read the middle row again. A partner airline's first class passenger may bring a guest into a United Polaris lounge; United's own Polaris passenger may not. That is not an error in this article; it is what United's table says.
United's arrivals lounge at London Heathrow is, in United's words, "exclusively for United Polaris travelers," positioned "right after going through immigration and customs," with showers and breakfast. It requires "a same-day ticket arriving before noon," and no guests are permitted.
The line that matters most: "A United Club membership or a one-time pass doesn't give you access to this lounge."
So the most valuable lounge in the network for a jet-lagged arrival is the one that money cannot open. It is a cabin benefit only, and it has a clock on it. A flight landing at 12:30pm does not qualify.
United lists a fourth category alongside the clubs and the Polaris lounges, and it is worth knowing exists so that you recognize the name at an airport.
United Club Fly appears in the same access sentence as the standard clubs: "Premium cabin customers on select flights, one-time passholders, United Club members, and eligible Star Gold members can access United Club locations and United Club Fly."
So the access routes are the same as the standard club. It is a different format rather than a different entitlement, and a membership or a qualifying boarding pass reaches both. If your airport has one rather than a full United Club, nothing about your eligibility changes.
United maintains the current list of every location, by airport and concourse, on its own site. That list is the thing to check before a trip, because clubs open, close and relocate within terminals more often than any guide gets updated.
Worth a paragraph, because a lounge network is only worth what it covers on the routes you fly.
United's clubs cluster at its hubs: Chicago O'Hare, Denver, Houston, Newark, San Francisco, Washington Dulles and Los Angeles carry multiple locations between them, and the international gateways carry the Polaris lounges. Away from that spine, coverage thins quickly, and a great many US airports that United serves have no United Club at all. Our list of Dulles Airport lounges shows what a United hub offers.
That geography is the argument for checking your own airports before buying anything. A membership that covers 45-plus locations covers none of them if your regular route is between two cities where United does not operate a club, and a lounge you cannot reach is worth exactly nothing regardless of the network's size.
It is also the argument that most often favors an issuer network over an airline one. United's clubs are United's; an independent network's lounges are wherever that network has signed a contract, which is a different and usually larger map. Neither is better in the abstract. The one that covers the airports you actually use is better.
One detail in the United Club table deserves separating, because it is unusually generous and almost nobody uses it.
Both the Polaris row and the United Business row grant access at "departure, connecting and arrival airports." That is an arrival entitlement on the same day you land, and it is not something every airline offers.
For a long-haul arrival into a United hub with a long domestic connection, that turns the wait into something considerably better than a gate seat. It also applies at the end of the journey, not only in the middle: land at your final destination and the club is still open to you that day.
The Heathrow arrivals lounge is the specialized version of the same idea, with tighter rules. The general arrival privilege at United Clubs has no noon cutoff and no separate ticket requirement beyond the one you already flew on.
You fly United long-haul in Polaris a few times a year. The lounges come with the ticket, and the only thing to verify is the fare bucket. Do not buy a membership for this; it duplicates what the fare already carries, and it will not open the Polaris lounge or Heathrow arrivals anyway.
You fly United domestically and often. This is the membership case, and the question is Individual against All Access. If you always travel alone, Individual at $750 is the whole decision and status will not move the price. If you travel with a partner, All Access is the only option that admits them, and United lists it at $1,400 at base status against $750 for Individual.
You fly a mix of airlines. United's network is 45-plus locations. An issuer network reaches considerably more, and it works on other carriers. Our Priority Pass guide covers the largest of them, and the Chase and Amex lounge comparison covers the two biggest US issuer networks. Buying an airline-specific membership while flying several airlines is the most common expensive mistake in this category.
Your Polaris fare bucket. Base gets the United Club. Standard and flexible get the Polaris lounge. Confirm it before you count on the lounge.
Whether your companion has their own access. No guests on almost every fare-based route, and none at all on Individual membership or a one-time pass.
The three-hour window on one-time passes. Since May 1, 2025, a pass works only within three hours of departure, unless you are connecting.
The Heathrow arrival time. Before noon, Polaris ticket, no guests, and no membership route in.
